# v2.5 scoping — round 3 premise: the DEV / PRODUCT boundary

> **Why this round exists.** Rounds 1–2 (`v2.5-scoping-DEBATE-OUTCOME.md`) converged on a plan whose
> single largest judgement — *import zero lifecycle artifacts* — rested on the claim **"GSD already
> owns the lifecycle."** That claim is true of the **development environment** and false of the
> **product**. The repo owner has now stated the boundary explicitly. Everything the panel derived
> from that claim must be re-derived.
>
> **Read `v2.5-scoping-BRIEF.md` (esp. §0) and `v2.5-scoping-DEBATE-OUTCOME.md` first.** This file is
> an addendum, not a replacement: every agreement recorded there stands unless the corrected premise
> actually overturns it. Say which agreements it overturns, and do not silently re-litigate the rest.

---

## 1. The boundary, stated by the owner

| | **DEV environment** | **PRODUCT (the harness being built)** |
|---|---|---|
What it is | this repo, where the harness is authored and maintained | the artifact set the harness ships into a *target monorepo* |
Runtime | Claude Code (+ Codex workers via Orca) | **opencode** (primary target) |
Lifecycle governance | **GSD** (`.claude/get-shit-done/`, `.planning/**`, `/gsd:*`) | **nothing today** |
Who drives it | the owner + top-tier models | a **weaker in-house model** + the owner |
Ships to the target repo? | **NO** — GSD is dev-only and is not emitted by `tools.harness_emit` | yes, this is the deliverable |

**Consequence the panel missed.** "GSD owns the lifecycle" cannot justify deleting lifecycle from the
product, because GSD is never present in the product. Under round 2's plan the shipped harness has
**no** task lifecycle, no routing, and no completion discipline of any kind — while its intended
driver is the weakest model in the picture. Round 2 §6.7 recorded this as accepted residual risk
("the weaker in-house model gets no flow router — it relies on the simplified existing `orchestrator`
plus GSD"). That sentence is now known to be **wrong on its facts**: GSD is not there.

**What does NOT change.** The heavy machinery is still deletable. A lifecycle is *procedure*; what
v2.5 deletes is the *verification apparatus* around procedure — schemas, CAS, attestation, a state
machine, capability allowlists, shadow metrics. Deleting the apparatus while shipping a light
procedure is coherent. §0 still binds: the product surface may not grow without retiring at least as
much, and nothing may be added to prove the harness cannot bypass itself.
